Why we are doing this

Five hundred people already do this by hand.

There is a WhatsApp group in Panama with around 500 surfers in it. All day they post photos and clips of the waves at different beaches so everyone knows which spot is worth the drive. That group is the best surf forecast in the country right now.

It exists because forecasts lie

The tracker says clean, 1.5 metres, light offshore. You drive two hours. It is blown out and unsurfable. Every surfer has this story. The models are not lying exactly, they just cannot see what happens in the last few hundred metres before a wave breaks.

But a chat group has no memory

Photos scroll past and vanish. Nothing accumulates. In a year that group will know exactly as much as it knows today. Every good observation is spent once and then lost.

The group is a sensor network that nobody is recording. That is the entire opportunity.

What we are building

An instrument, not a beach postcard.

Four layers. Only one of them is AI, and it is not the one that makes the forecast accurate. Getting that split right is the difference between a real product and a demo.

Layer 01 / Data

Read everything

Four independent global wave models, real tide gauges, real airport wind. Pulled every hour. Written down permanently and never overwritten.

Layer 02 / Physics

Score the break

Plain arithmetic per spot. Does the swell fit this break, is the wind actually offshore for this shore angle, is the tide in range. No AI, fully explainable.

Layer 03 / Learning

Learn the error

People report what it was really like. We join that back to what each model predicted and learn how each one is wrong at each specific break.

Layer 04 / Language

Make the call

The AI writes the daily call, explains why, reads photos, and says plainly when it does not know. It narrates. It does not compute the score.

The hard constraint

There is no wave buoy anywhere near Panama's Pacific coast. We checked the full station registry directly rather than trusting search results. Every buoy between Ecuador and Colombia is dead, was never instrumented for waves, or has literally been adrift since June 2025. Nearest working one is 680km away on the wrong coast.

Webcams do not save us either. Two of sixteen named spots have any camera at all, one was offline, and their terms forbid automated capture. Satellite cannot resolve a wave.

Which is why this works

If there were buoys everywhere, this would be a data plumbing exercise and Surfline would already have done it. There are none. The only possible ground truth in Panama is people.

You have 500 of them, already posting daily, already organised. The hardest part of any crowdsourced product is the cold start, and it is already solved.

Layer 02 — The physics

Wind counts as much as size. That is the whole lesson.

When the tracker says perfect and the beach is garbage, the size was usually right. The wind is what ruined it. Any scoring that treats wind as a minor modifier reproduces the exact failure this thing exists to fix.

Four sub-scores, one number

  • Swell direction is a gate, not a weight. If the swell cannot physically reach the break, nothing else matters and the score collapses.
  • Effective size is period-weighted, never raw height. A wave carries energy proportional to height squared times period.
  • Wind is measured along the shore normal. Onshore kills a spot roughly twice as fast as too-much-offshore does, so the penalty is deliberately asymmetric.
  • Tide uses a narrow window for a shallow reef and a wide one for a beachbreak. One number per spot encodes "this only works on the push".

Every sub-score is kept and shown. So the page can always answer why: Wind 0.18. That is what killed it.

Both read "1.5 m" on any forecast app. The long-period one is a completely different wave. Scoring raw height instead of energy is the single most common way a forecast misleads you.

Layer 03 — The learning loop

Ten to thirty reports. That is the whole ballgame.

This is the number everything else hangs on. It came out of the maths, not a guess, and it is far smaller than it has any right to be.

Each turn of this loop permanently improves the next forecast at that break.

Why the number is so small

Most of a model's error at a given break is not random. It is a persistent offset caused by something physical and unchanging: the reef refracts swell a certain way, an island shadows it, the model grid cell sits in water too deep to see the bank. That offset repeats every single time.

You do not need to learn chaos. You need to measure a constant. And measuring a constant through noise takes far fewer samples than people expect.

Reports at a spotWhat unlocks
10 to 30Correction starts beating the raw model. First honest accuracy claim.
~60The offset is pinned to within 10 centimetres.
100 to 200It learns when a spot fails, not just that it does. Probably the best forecast available for that break, anywhere.

Ten to thirty reports is one season with a small local crew. Not a research programme.

The decision we cannot get wrong

Every prediction has to be written down at the moment it is made, from day one. Forecast archives are not retrievable after the fact. You cannot go back and ask what the model said last Tuesday. If we overwrite yesterday's forecast with today's, there is nothing to compare reality against and the entire learning loop is impossible, forever. It costs almost nothing to do and it cannot be added later. This is the one thing that has to be right in version one.

Why global makes it smarter

A new break inherits what similar breaks already learned.

Panama is the beachhead, not the boundary. Nothing gets hardcoded to it. And going wide is not just a bigger audience, it materially improves the forecast for every individual spot.

Model error is regional

A global wave model does not fail randomly at one beach. It tends to fail the same way along a whole stretch of coast, for shared physical reasons. So a correction learned at one break genuinely transfers to its neighbours.

Zero reports still gets a forecast

A brand new break with no data inherits a correction from structurally similar breaks: same break type, same exposure, same shore orientation. It starts smarter than raw model output on its first day.

More spots, better priors

Every region added sharpens the shared baseline that new spots borrow from. Panama alone, the flywheel is weak. Worldwide, it compounds. The dataset itself becomes worth publishing openly.

Layer 04 — Where the AI actually belongs

It explains the forecast. It does not invent it.

Worth being blunt about this, because getting it backwards is how these projects die. If the language model disappeared tomorrow, the app would be less pleasant to use and exactly as accurate.

What it is genuinely best at

  • Adjudicating disagreement. When four models say four different things, turning that into one plain sentence with a confidence level.
  • Reading photos. Judging size against the surfer in frame, wind texture on the face, how crowded it is.
  • Encoding local knowledge. "Venao goes to pieces when the wind swings southwest after 10am" is a rule a person knows and a model does not.
  • Explaining why. Not just a score, but what drove it and what would have to change.

What it must never be asked to do

  • Compute the score. Language models are bad at arithmetic and worse at pretending to be. The score is deterministic and auditable.
  • Predict wave height. That is what the physics models are for. We correct them, we do not replace them.
  • Run on page load. Generating per visitor turns a $0.21 monthly bill into $345. It runs once on a schedule, and the result is a static file.
  • Sound confident when it is not. Uncertainty is reported beside the score, never quietly folded into it.
We are not predicting waves. We are correcting a physical model's known local errors and explaining its uncertainty. That is a truer story, and still an impressive one.

What we will and will not claim

The accuracy scorecard is the actual product.

Not one competitor publishes how accurate their forecast turned out to be. Not Surfline, not Windguru, none of them. It is the one genuinely unoccupied space in this market, and it is the thing a group of 500 surfers will instantly respect.

Say this

"Learns your local break from the people who surf it."

"For Santa Catalina, GFS-Wave has run 25% too big for the last 30 days. We are discounting it."

"Models disagree by 40% today and nobody has reported since Tuesday. Low confidence."

Never say this

"Perfect." Not reachable, and the claim is self-harming. It sets up exactly the broken promise that made surfers distrust forecasts in the first place.

"AI predicts the waves." It does not.

Any accuracy claim on day one. There is no data yet. The claim becomes earnable at 10 to 30 reports per spot, and not before.

The honest ceiling

Meaningfully better than any single forecast at the spots our community actually reports on. And never better than the disagreement between two good surfers watching the same session.

How it gets built

Precompute the whole world. Serve flat files.

One scheduled job per hour reads every source, scores every spot, and writes the entire site out as static JSON. The CDN serves those files. No database on the read path. No compute per visitor. It is the cheapest possible shape for something read constantly and written rarely.

Per-spot corrections go live once a spot crosses its threshold. The public scorecard turns on. This is the moment the product becomes something no competitor has.

Phase 4

Everywhere else

Spots are data, not code, so a new country is a data import. Corrections learned in Panama transfer to structurally similar breaks worldwide.

What this is for

Free, open, and built to be argued with.

No ads, no subscription, no premium tier. The code is public and so is the accuracy record. If the forecast is wrong, the site says so before anyone else has to point it out.

That last part is the whole ethic. Every existing surf forecast asks you to trust it and gives you no way to check. This one keeps the receipts and shows them, and gets better specifically because the people using it push back.
